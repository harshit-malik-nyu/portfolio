Synthetic demonstrations, not observed experiments. Every preset is an invented test fixture. The Retailrocket study is observational and contains no randomized assignments or harm outcomes. No treatment effect or realized return is claimed here.

1. Inspect the inputs

One visitor, one arm, binary purchase within seven days. Harm is a separate binary outcome over the same window. Planned allocation: 50:50.

2. Verify design requirements

These switches demonstrate required evidence. The tool cannot verify identity, assignment logs or a preregistration from aggregate counts.

3. Challenge the recommendation

The conversion lower bound must exceed the economic break-even lift. The harm upper bound must stay below the prespecified ceiling. A sample ratio mismatch (p < 0.001), incomplete follow-up, unverified design or sparse outcomes blocks interpretation.

Intervals use four Bonferroni-adjusted Wilson bounds for approximate joint 95% coverage under independent visitors. This is deliberately conservative, not an exact guarantee or a sequential test. The sparse-outcome gate is a practical screening rule, not a coverage proof.

Dollar outputs assume one incremental order per incremental purchaser, constant margin and order value, and a future audience comparable to the experiment. They exclude repeat orders, uncertain costs and changing order mix. Negative lower-bound value means the assumed investment case has not cleared the gate.
